How to read the numbers

Ohio high school · statewide

Rankings

Rating is how strong a wrestler is right now, built from every bout we have; the ± is how sure it is. P4P Score puts every weight on one scale: 500 is a typical state qualifier, 1,000 a typical state champion.

How rankings work

Ratings use Glicko-2: every bout moves both wrestlers, and a pin moves them more than a decision. The ± shows how sure the rating is. To be ranked a wrestler needs an Ohio team and 6 rated bouts in the season (4 for weekly rankings). Early in the season, until December 15, the weekly list also shows returning wrestlers after 1 bout and new wrestlers after 3, marked provisional, so there is a current list from the first weekend. Ratings compare wrestlers within a season; P4P Scores compare across weights and seasons. Records count only bouts in the database. How accurate are the ratings?

Grade comes from the OHSAA state pairings, middle school records and full four-season careers, so it is known for about half of ranked wrestlers. Wrestlers without a known grade are left out when a grade is picked.

Provisional Early in the season (until December 15) a returning wrestler is listed after 1 bout and a new wrestler after 3, so there is a current list from the first weekend. Ranks firm up once a wrestler has 4 bouts.

How the odds work

Brackets use the real draw (bracket spots 1 to 16 from the OHSAA pairings). Each bout's chance comes from the blend: the wrestler's rating plus their official season record, district finish, grade and last year's state finish, weighted by what predicted state bouts best in earlier seasons, with recent seasons counting most. The more of both wrestlers' bouts we have, the more the rating counts. Title odds follow the bracket exactly; placing odds approximate the consolation crossings.

Each box is one group of four bracket spots: two first-round bouts, whose winners meet in the quarterfinal for one semifinal spot. Each half of the bracket sends one wrestler to the final.

How it works: each bout's odds come from the two wrestlers' latest ratings for the season, and the way a win comes (fall, tech fall, major, decision) follows how bouts with the same odds have gone in our results. Lineups start with each team's highest-rated wrestler at his or her usual weight; change any spot. Duals are worked out exactly over every possible result of every bout, so the same lineups always give the same odds; fall, forfeit or default 6, tech fall 5, major 4, decision 3. Key bouts are the close ones that change a team's chance to win the dual the most. Tournaments: seeded by rating; 2 points per championship win and 1 per consolation win, bonus 2 for a fall, 1.5 for a tech fall, 1 for a major, and placement points 16-12-10-9-7-6-4-3.

How the test works

  • Blend is the rating plus each wrestler's official season record, district finish, grade and last year's state finish. Its weights are learned only from earlier seasons, with recent seasons counting most, and the rating counts for more when we have more of both wrestlers' bouts.
  • Rating alone uses only bout results: every bout moves both wrestlers. A pin, tech fall or major counts for more than a close decision, and forfeits, injury defaults and byes don't count.
  • Record and District are simple guesses for comparison: pick the wrestler with the better season record, or the better district finish.
  • Regular-season bouts count half as much as postseason bouts. Ratings carry into the next season with more uncertainty. 2019-20 had no state tournament.
